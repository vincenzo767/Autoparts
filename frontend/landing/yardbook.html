Chapter I

TheHalf-Cut

It starts at the port. Containers arrive from Japan and South Korea packed with half-cuts — the front ends of cars and vans, engines still in place, cut clean behind the front seats.

Each one has a history: a low-mileage commuter, a delivery van, a family SUV. We read the plates and the service stickers, note what came in, and roll them into the yard to wait their turn.

Chapter II

On theBench

Nothing goes on the shelf untested. Engines are turned over and run, alternators are spun up and measured, starters are cranked against a load. Bearings are felt by hand for roughness; calipers are pushed until the pistons move freely.

Then comes the grade. Grade A for parts that are clean and fully working, Grade B for honest wear or cosmetic marks. Tires get a tread percentage. If a part doesn't pass, it doesn't get sold.

Chapter III

TheHeart

Engines are the heart of the yard, and the heaviest promise we make.

A 2KD from a Japanese Hilux, a 4D56 from a Strada, a D4CB from a Korean Starex — each is lifted out whole, drained, cleaned and run before it's offered. We keep the injectors, turbo and harness with the block wherever we can, so the swap on your side is as straight as possible.

Chapter IV

SecondRoad

The last chapter happens in someone else's garage. A mechanic bolts the part in, turns the key, and a vehicle that was off the road is back on it.

That's the whole point of surplus: good parts, already built once, given a second road at a price that keeps Cebu moving.
